Testimonial: The Arranged Weekend
James C., 52, Corporate Litigator, Chicago
Kowloon, March 2023
I paid a fixer in the Expat Bar Association two thousand USD to arrange a woman for Friday night through Sunday morning—discreet, no strings, just company for a lonely divorced man who didn't want to navigate Tinder at my age. What I got was Joyce.
She arrived at the hotel Friday at 8 PM wearing a fitted red dress that showed the scar on her knee when she walked. She was reserved at first, setting her small bag down with precision, but when I moved to kiss her hello, she caught my bottom lip between her teeth and sucked on it—slow, deliberate, filthy in the sweetest way. It made me wild. I tossed her onto the bed without thinking, and she laughed into my mouth, her dark eyes catching the light. Afterward, she called it "making love," which was generous of her, considering I'd paid for her time.
I knew this was transactional. She knew I knew. But she still treated it like a relationship she was trying to save. She did my laundry Saturday morning while I watched basketball—she actually understood the pick-and-roll, shouted at the screen when the Warriors missed a three. She cooked me Filipino food in that same red dress, now wrinkled from sleep, and didn't flinch when I spilled sauce on the carpet or when she saw the pill bottles on the nightstand. That tolerance for gross realities—my aging body, the transactional nature of our arrangement, the way I sometimes forget to wash—made it feel strangely domestic.
She let me caress her Filipino legs while we watched TV, her olive skin warm under my hands. I noticed the scar on the side of her nose when she turned her head to laugh at something on the screen. Sunday morning, she packed my suitcase with the same efficiency she applied to everything, folding my dirty clothes alongside the clean ones, placing a pressed rose I'd bought her Saturday between my shirts like a bookmark. She kissed me goodbye at the door, soft and closed-mouth this time, and I handed her an envelope with the rest of her fee. She took it without looking inside, smiled with that scar catching the hallway light, and walked toward the elevator. I stood in the doorway wondering which one of us had been more lonely before she arrived.
